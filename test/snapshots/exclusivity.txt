  CAVEAT (tell the user): Within “NSCLC”, over its 1,668 samples with a gene panel (a sample without a panel record is left out, never read as wild-type), counting any alteration (mutation, amplification or deep deletion, structural variant). DISCOVER's background gives each sample and gene a probability of alteration, σ(a_sample + b_gene), fitted so that every sample's expected number of altered genes is its observed burden and every gene's expected number of altered samples its observed count — over the whole altered-gene matrix (410 genes, each sample's own panels: a gene off a sample's panel has no probability there); the fit matched every margin to within 0.01% in 3 rounds. For a pair, the number of samples altered in both under independence given those probabilities is Poisson-binomial with p_i = p_iA·p_iB, over the samples profiled for both; exclusivity is its lower tail at the observed count, co-occurrence its upper tail, each Benjamini–Hochberg across the 15 pairs, all exact. Beside it, the plain two-sided Fisher test on the same samples, which assumes every tumour is equally likely to carry each alteration — heavily altered tumours then manufacture co-occurrence and lightly altered ones exclusivity: here 9 pairs are exclusive at DISCOVER q < 0.05 against 7 by Fisher. Exclusivity is a pattern, not a mechanism: synthetic lethality, redundant pathway hits, and subtypes with different drivers (the confounder stratifying removes, when the strata are the right ones) all produce it.
